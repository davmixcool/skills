# Launch Playbook

## Phase 1 — Validate before building

Create:
- product name
- one-line promise
- 3–5 screenshots/mockups
- simple landing page
- waitlist or preorder signal
- 3 ad concepts

Run a small traffic test if practical.

Look for:
- click-through interest
- landing-page conversion
- comments/questions indicating real pain
- willingness to pay

## Phase 2 — Build V1

V1 should include only:
- onboarding
- add first entity
- generated/suggested events
- notifications
- completion/reschedule
- paywall
- analytics

Avoid feature creep.

## Phase 3 — Internal reliability test

Test:
- app killed
- phone restarted
- timezone changed
- notification permission disabled/re-enabled
- daylight saving where applicable
- event edited
- event deleted
- subscription restored
- app upgraded

Reminder reliability must be treated like payments reliability.

## Phase 4 — Store launch

Prepare:
- app icon
- screenshots
- short subtitle
- keyword-aware description
- privacy policy
- terms
- support page/email
- subscription disclosures

## Phase 5 — Initial acquisition

Use direct pain hooks.

Examples:

### Car
> When did you last change your transmission fluid?

### Expiry
> Your passport will not remind you before it expires.

### Subscription
> That free trial you forgot is about to charge you.

### Freelancer
> How many invoices have you forgotten to follow up?

## Phase 6 — Decide

After enough qualified usage, classify the app:

### Kill
Weak activation + weak retention + weak payment intent.

### Maintain
Small profitable utility with low support burden.

### Grow
Strong paid economics. Invest in ads, ASO and adjacent features.

### Graduate
Strong retention and expansion demand. Turn it into a larger standalone business.

## Expansion gate

Do not add major complexity until you can answer yes to most of:
- users complete setup
- reminders are acted on
- users retain the app
- users pay
- users ask for deeper workflows
- acquisition can be repeated economically
