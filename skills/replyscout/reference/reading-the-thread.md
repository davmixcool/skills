# Reading the thread

Read every reply before drafting. Fill this in, in your head or in the brief.

## 1. The post

- **Who:** handle, and roughly how big (followers, if you have them). Under a very
  large account, replies drown unless they're early and good.
- **What kind of post:** a joke, a complaint, a question, a hot take, a launch, an
  announcement, someone venting. A joke wants a joke or a sharp fact, not an essay.
  A question wants an answer.
- **How old, how many replies:** the timing label sums it up:
  - **hot:** under about 3 hours with up to about 60 replies, or the author is
    still replying (up to about 12 hours);
  - **late:** over a day old, or over 200 replies;
  - **warm:** everything in between.

## 2. The register

What does a normal reply here look like?

- Length: one line, or paragraphs?
- Tone: sarcastic, earnest, technical, supportive, angry?
- Style: lowercase, emoji, swearing, memes?

Write the drafts to sit naturally among them. A polished, capitalised paragraph
in a thread of one-liners reads as a bot.

## 3. What's already been said

List the points made, even roughly: "if everything is 5 stars nothing is",
"Goodhart's law", "only book 4.9+". A draft that makes any of them again is out,
however it's worded.

## 4. What's working

- Which reply has the most likes, and why? Firsthand experience ("as an Airbnb
  host…") usually beats opinion.
- Is the author replying to anyone? Then replies under theirs are being read, and
  they may answer you.
- Is anyone's reply turning into a conversation? That sub-thread is where the
  attention is.

## 5. The gap

One sentence: what has nobody said? This is the angle for the first draft. For
example: "Nobody has brought another platform with the same problem", "Nobody has
explained *why* it happens", "Nobody has asked the host what they'd change".

If there's no gap, say so and suggest skipping. Not every thread needs the maker.
