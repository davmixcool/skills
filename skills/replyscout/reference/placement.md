# Where to reply

The same reply gets very different attention depending on where it goes.

| Where | When it's best | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Under the post** | Early (few replies), or when the gap is a direct answer to the post | Replies to the post are ranked by the platform. Early and specific rises. |
| **Under the top reply** | The thread already has a clear top reply, and you can build on it | Everyone who opens the thread reads the top reply first. Its author, often smaller and more likely to answer, gets your notification. |
| **Under the author's own reply** | The author is answering in the thread | The author is notified of replies to their reply, and that sub-thread is where they're paying attention. |
| **Under a reply that's turning into a conversation** | Two people are going back and forth on something you know | Join a live exchange instead of starting a cold one. |

## Rules of thumb

- **Build on, don't compete with.** Under the top reply, add to its point (a fact, an
  example), don't repeat it or argue with it unless you have a real counterpoint.
- **Big accounts:** a reply under the post competes with hundreds. A reply under the
  top reply, from someone who'll answer, often goes further.
- **At most two replies from the maker in one thread,** in different sub-threads.
  More looks like farming attention.
- **Say where and why in the placement,** with the URL when it's not the post itself:
  the extension opens that reply for Open & fill.

## Example (5 Oct 2026)

levelsio's "Airbnb's entire rating scale is now 4.5 to 5.0" had 27 replies, and
the top one was an Airbnb host (@ojm, 810 followers) saying it might as well be
thumbs up/down. The maker replied **under @ojm**, adding a fact nobody had
mentioned (Netflix switched from 5 stars to thumbs in 2017). @ojm answered within
four minutes, and the maker's reply sat second in the thread's most-read
sub-thread.
