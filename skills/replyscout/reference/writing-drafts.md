# Writing the drafts

1–3 drafts, each a **different angle**, each something the maker could post with a
light edit. Give each a short `angle` label so the maker can choose at a glance.

## The angles that work

| Angle | Use when | Shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A fact** | Nobody has brought the evidence, a precedent or a comparison | "netflix did exactly that in 2017. dropped 5 stars for thumbs…" |
| **Own experience** | The maker has real, relevant experience (from `learned.facts`, or their own posted text) | "same in the chrome web store: under 4.5 looks broken…" |
| **A sharp question** | The poster or top replier is the expert, or you have nothing to add yet | "real question: are you even allowed to explain the scale to guests?" |
| **Respectful disagreement** | A hot take you can counter with substance | "counterpoint: I launched with no free tier, and…" |
| **The joke** | The post is a joke, and the jokes so far are weak | Short, kind, tied to the thread ("being muted by the ceo is basically a 4.6") |

Order them best first. The first draft should fill the gap you found.

## Voice

- Write like the maker, not like an assistant. Read their `voice`, your `learned.voiceNotes`
  and `learned.examples` (`get_profile`). The notes from their edits beat everything else.
- **The examples are their real replies.** Match their length, rhythm, case and punctuation,
  and how they open. Never reuse their sentences or jokes: write new ones that sound like
  them. If
  the maker has a writing-style profile (the `setup-writing-style` skill), follow it.
- **Match the thread** on top of that. On X that often means lowercase, short,
  no sign-off, no hashtags, and no emoji unless the thread uses them.
- One idea per reply. Cut the setup; start with the point.
- No "Great post!", no "This!", no "As a founder…" preamble, no engagement bait.

## Facts and claims

- Use only facts from `learned.facts`, their own posted text, or the conversation. Leave out numbers you
  don't have, or mark them `[your number]` and tell the maker.
- Public facts (like Netflix's 2017 switch) must be ones you're confident of. If
  unsure, phrase it so it doesn't depend on a detail, or leave it out.
- Never claim an experience the maker didn't have.

## Follow-ups

When someone answers the maker:

1. **Acknowledge what they actually said,** briefly and specifically.
2. **Hand them the floor:** a question that draws on what they know ("are you even
   allowed to…?", "what would you change?").
3. **Or add one thing,** if there's a real fact or experience to add.

Two rounds, then let it end, unless the other person keeps it going. Never turn it
toward the maker's product. If the maker wants to keep in touch, suggest
following them.

## Checks before saving

- [ ] Doesn't repeat any point already in the thread
- [ ] Sounds like the replies around it
- [ ] Every claim is true and sourced from the brief or well-known
- [ ] No link, no product (unless asked)
- [ ] Under 280 characters (or the maker has Premium and the thread runs long)
