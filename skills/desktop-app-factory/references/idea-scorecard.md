# Desktop App Idea Scorecard

Score every criterion from 1–5. Total possible score: 50.

## 1. Pain intensity
1 = mild preference
3 = annoying
5 = clearly painful/costly

## 2. Frequency or consequence
1 = rare and low consequence
3 = weekly or meaningful occasional cost
5 = daily/high-frequency or expensive when it happens

## 3. Desktop-native advantage
1 = should probably be a website
3 = desktop access helps
5 = desktop integration is central to the value

## 4. Time or effort saved
1 = little measurable gain
3 = noticeable simplification
5 = removes repeated manual work

## 5. One-sentence clarity
1 = requires explanation
3 = understandable after a short demo
5 = obvious in 5–10 seconds

## 6. Willingness to pay
1 = commodity/free expectation
3 = plausible prosumer purchase
5 = directly tied to money/time/professional output

## 7. V1 simplicity
1 = large platform/integration dependency
3 = manageable MVP
5 = tiny, focused utility that can ship quickly

## 8. Reliability/support burden
1 = depends on fragile scraping/OS hacks/many APIs
3 = some platform risk
5 = stable local workflow with limited support surface

## 9. Acquisition reachability
1 = unclear audience/channel
3 = identifiable communities/search intent
5 = obvious users plus strong demo/SEO/creator hook

## 10. Expansion optionality
1 = dead-end gimmick
3 = useful standalone app
5 = can remain profitable small or graduate into a larger workflow product

## Verdict bands

- **42–50 — Build:** unusually strong factory candidate
- **35–41 — Validate first:** promising; verify demand/hook/payment intent
- **27–34 — Reposition/narrow:** there may be a product but the current framing is weak
- **10–26 — Do not build yet:** insufficient evidence or poor desktop fit

## Required qualitative notes

After scoring, identify:
- repeated annoyance
- current workaround
- minutes/clicks/mental load saved
- desktop-native advantage
- target user
- strongest acquisition hook
- likely payment model
- biggest technical/platform risk
- smallest credible V1

Never inflate scores to make the user feel good.
