# Writing the messages

Each message is from one person to another, about the other person's work. It's short, specific,
and easy to say no to.

## A first email

Subject: specific and modest. Good: `A Mac app for your next round-up, if it earns a place`,
`A Dock app for your next "Mac apps" video`, `Shortcuts, one flick from the cursor`.
Never: "Quick question", "Collaboration opportunity", "Partnership", anything in capitals.

Body, 100–180 words:

1. **Their work, by name.** `I watched "<exact title>"` only if the person actually did; otherwise
   `I saw "<exact title>"` or `Your "<exact title>" video, with <what was in it>`. One concrete
   detail from it (a product they featured, the format). Link it in `sources`, not in the email.
2. **What the product is, in two sentences,** in plain words, ending on why it fits *their*
   audience.
3. **The demo:** one link, under two minutes.
4. **The trial and the offer:** `It comes with <trial>, so you can try everything. If you'd like
   to keep it, just reply and I'll send you a free licence.`
5. **No strings:** `No strings: if it's not for you, no need to reply.`
6. **Sign-off:** first name, then the product's plain address (`example.com`, no tracking).

Example:

> Hi Maya,
>
> I saw your "9 Mac Apps I Use Every Day" video, and liked that you showed each one in use
> rather than reading out a list. I make a small Mac app called Beckon, and I think it might
> fit a future round-up.
>
> It puts your Dock in a ring around the cursor: hold ⌥Space, flick toward an app, let go. Its
> windows fan out with live previews, and if you drag a file to the ring you can convert or
> compress it on the way.
>
> 90-second demo: https://youtu.be/…
>
> It comes with 14 days of Pro, so you can try everything. If you'd like to keep it, just reply
> and I'll send you a free licence. No strings: if it's not for you, no need to reply.
>
> Thanks,
> David
> withbeckon.app

## A first DM

40–80 words, no subject, the same order compressed:

> Hi Leo, I like your "Mac apps worth installing" lists. I make Beckon: your Dock in a ring
> around the cursor (hold ⌥Space, flick to an app), and it converts files on the way. 90-sec
> demo: <link>. It comes with 14 days of Pro, and if you'd like to keep it, I'll send you a free
> licence. No strings.

If the person also emailed, the DM backup says so: `I emailed <address> too.`

## Other languages

Write in the contact's language when you can write it naturally; keep product names and
keyboard shortcuts as they are. If you can't, write in plain, simple English (most creators read
it). Don't machine-translate a template word for word: rewrite it.

## Long shots

The same message, a little shorter, with a code to fill in (`[FREE LICENCE CODE]`) because a
reply is unlikely. Lead with the one feature most likely to interest *them* (a creator known
for automation hears about the automation).

## Directory and launch-site submissions

Not a message: the form's fields. Write them from the brief:

- tagline (often 60–180 characters), a one-line description, the full description (what it does,
  key features, who it's for, pricing), categories, pricing model and starting price, platforms
  and requirements, the demo video, screenshots (real ones, the product in use);
- review notes, if asked: how to test it without paying (the trial), the permissions it asks
  for and why, a contact for a full licence.

## Newsletters and press

A factual pitch: a subject with the product's name and one line, then five bullets (what it
is, the key features, platforms, price, the maker), the site, the demo, and "happy to answer
anything or send a licence". Attach two or three real screenshots when emailing press.

## Never write

- That anyone watched, read, used or loved something they didn't.
- Hype: "game-changer", "revolutionary", "the best", "10x".
- "I'd love for you to review it", "would you feature us", or any offer of payment.
- More than two links, or a key, code or tracked link in a first message (see the skill's rules).
- Fake familiarity ("Hey buddy"), guilt ("just following up again"), or deadlines.

## Placeholders

Anything the person must fill in goes in square brackets in capitals: `[FREE LICENCE CODE]`,
`[MACNATIVE LINK]`, `[THEIR NAME]`; `[name]` for the contact's name in reply templates. The page
highlights them, and the builder lists them.
