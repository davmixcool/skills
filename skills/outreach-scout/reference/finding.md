# Finding the right people

Look for people who already talk to the product's audience about this kind of thing. A creator
who reviewed a direct competitor last month beats one with ten times the audience who never has.

## The searches that work

Run these through web search (and on YouTube's own search results pages when you can read
them). Replace `<category>` with how people search for the product ("menu bar app", "tab
manager", "screen recorder"), and `<competitor>` with the two or three best-known alternatives.

- `best <category> apps <this year>` and `<platform> apps you need <this year>`
- `<competitor> review`, `<competitor> vs`, `<competitor> alternative`
- `site:youtube.com <competitor>` and `site:youtube.com "<platform> apps" <this year>`
- `<category> newsletter`, `site:substack.com <category>`, `<platform> apps weekly`
- `<category> podcast episode`, `"<competitor>" podcast`
- `"<competitor>"` in other languages' words for "review" or "alternative" (`alternativa`,
  `alternative à`, `Alternative zu`): non-English creators reply more often

Keep anything from the last 12 months; older only if the creator is still active.

## Where to look, by product type

Check each site is live and how it accepts submissions before recommending it: directories come
and go, and some now charge.

**Mac apps**
- YouTube: "Mac apps I can't live without", "best Mac apps <year>", "<competitor> review",
  menu bar and productivity setups. Plenty of non-English channels review Mac utilities.
- Launch sites and directories: MacNative, JustMacApps, Product Hunt, AlternativeTo (list it as
  an alternative to the competitors), MacUpdate, the `awesome-mac` list on GitHub (by pull
  request).
- Press: 9to5Mac's weekly Indie App Spotlight (tips by email), MacStories (tips), Mac podcasts
  that take listener tips.
- Communities to post in yourself, not pitch: r/macapps (developers may post their own apps,
  following its rules).

**Chrome extensions and browser tools**
- YouTube: "best Chrome extensions <year>", "extensions for <job>" (students, designers,
  developers), competitor reviews.
- Directories: Product Hunt, AlternativeTo, extension round-up sites in the category, AI
  directories if it's an AI tool.
- Communities: r/chrome_extensions, category subreddits (post, don't pitch).

**SaaS and web apps**
- YouTube and newsletters in the customer's job (marketing, finance, design), not "SaaS"
  in general.
- Launch sites: Product Hunt, BetaList, Uneed, Microlaunch, Peerlist Launchpad, Indie Hackers
  (a post), SaaSHub and AlternativeTo.

**Developer tools**
- Show HN (posted by the maker, Tuesday–Thursday morning US time), dev newsletters that take
  submissions, `awesome-<topic>` lists on GitHub, YouTube channels that review tools in that
  language or framework.

**Mobile apps**
- YouTube and TikTok creators in the app's category; App Store and Google Play featuring
  nominations (both stores have a form for developers); review sites in the category.

## Social accounts

X, LinkedIn, Instagram and TikTok mostly need a login to search: don't log in. Use web search
for their public posts (`"<platform> apps" site:x.com`), or ask the person for handles they
already know. Accounts that regularly post "apps worth installing" lists are good DMs.

## What to note for each candidate

Enough to qualify them (next step): their name, the link to the piece of work that fits, its
date, and the platform. Don't collect anything else yet.
